# 30 QUESTIONS
# Question 1. Elevator accelerating upwards

Given: u = 0 m/s, a = 0.80 m/s², t = 15 s.

(i) Final speed: v = u + at = 0 + (0.80)(15) = 12 m/s.
(ii) Average speed during uniform acceleration: v_avg = (u + v)/2 = (0 + 12)/2 = 6.0 m/s.
(iii) Distance: s = ut + ½at² = (0)(15) + ½(0.80)(15²) = 90 m.

**Answer: (i) 12 m/s; (ii) 6.0 m/s; (iii) 90 m.**

# Question 2. Hotel lift

Given: u = 0 m/s, a = 1.2 m/s², t = 10 s.

(i) v = u + at = 0 + (1.2)(10) = 12 m/s.
(ii) v_avg = (u + v)/2 = (0 + 12)/2 = 6.0 m/s.
(iii) s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½(1.2)(10²) = 60 m.

**Answer: (i) 12 m/s; (ii) 6.0 m/s; (iii) 60 m.**

# Question 3. Truck speed changes from 18 km/h to 72 km/h

Convert to SI units: u = 18 ÷ 3.6 = 5 m/s; v = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s; t = 15 s.

(a) Average speed = (u + v)/2 = (5 + 20)/2 = 12.5 m/s.
(b) Acceleration: a = (v − u)/t = (20 − 5)/15 = 1.0 m/s².
(c) Distance: s = v_avg t = (12.5)(15) = 187.5 m.

**Answer: (a) 12.5 m/s; (b) 1.0 m/s²; (c) 187.5 m.**

# Question 4. Car braking before an obstacle

0 = 400 − 10s, so s = 40 m.

The obstacle is 45 m away. The car needs 40 m to stop, which is less than 45 m.

**Answer: Yes. It stops 5 m before the obstacle (assuming braking starts immediately).**

# Question 5. Stone thrown vertically upwards

Given: initial velocity u = 25 m/s; acceleration a = −9.8 m/s².

(a) At the highest point, v = 0. From v = u + at: 0 = 25 − 9.8t, so t = 25/9.8 = 2.55 s.
(b) Maximum height: v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 25² − 2(9.8)h.
h = 625/19.6 = 31.9 m.
(c) Total flight time = 2 × time up = 2(2.55) = 5.10 s.

**Answer: (a) 2.55 s; (b) 31.9 m; (c) 5.10 s.**

# Question 7. Distance and displacement

> [!NOTE]
> Distance is the total length of the path travelled. It is a scalar and has magnitude only.
> 
> Displacement is the straight-line change from the starting point to the ending point. It is a vector and has magnitude and direction. They are not always equal because the path travelled can be longer than the straight-line change in position.
> 
> Example: walk 3 m east and then 3 m west. Distance = 6 m, but displacement = 0 m because you return to the starting point.
> 
> **Answer: Distance describes the whole path; displacement describes the net change in position.**

# Question 8. Acceleration due to gravity

Near Earth’s surface, g is treated as approximately constant because the distance from Earth’s centre changes very little for ordinary short motions. At the highest point of a vertically thrown object, its instantaneous velocity is 0, but gravity still acts.

**Answer: Acceleration = 9.8 m/s² downward at the highest point (air resistance neglected).**

# Question 9. Linear momentum and collisions

> [!NOTE]
> Linear momentum is p = mv, where m is mass and v is velocity. It is a vector because velocity has direction. Elastic collision: total momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved.
> Inelastic collision: total momentum is conserved in an isolated system, but kinetic energy is not conserved. In a perfectly inelastic collision, the objects stick together.
> 
> **Answer: Momentum is conserved in both elastic and inelastic collisions; kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic collisions.**

# Question 10. Law of conservation of linear momentum

> [!NOTE]
> Statement: If the net external force on a system is zero, the total linear momentum of the system remains constant.
> Total momentum before = Total momentum after
> 
> Example: when two skaters push away from each other, they move in opposite directions. Their momenta are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction if the system was initially at rest.
> 

**Answer: Momentum is conserved when the net external force is zero.**

# Question 11. Speed and velocity

Speed = distance ÷ time. It is a scalar and has magnitude only. Example: a cyclist travels at 10 m/s.
Velocity = displacement ÷ time. It is a vector and includes direction. Example: a cyclist travels at 10 m/s east.

**Answer: Speed has no direction; velocity includes direction.**

# Question 12. Impulse and impulse–momentum theorem

> [!NOTE]
> Impulse is the product of force and the time interval for which it acts: J = F_avg Δt.
> Impulse–momentum theorem: impulse equals the change in momentum, J = Δp = m(v − u).
> 
> Air-bags and crumple zones increase the time over which a person’s momentum changes. For the same change in momentum, a longer time means a smaller average force.

**Answer: Air-bags and crumple zones reduce the average force during a collision.**

# Question 13. Why momentum is conserved in an isolated system

For two bodies interacting, Newton’s third law gives F₁₂ = −F₂₁.
Using F = Δp/Δt: Δp₁/Δt = −Δp₂/Δt, so Δp₁ = −Δp₂.

Therefore, Δp₁ + Δp₂ = 0 and p₁ + p₂ stays constant, provided no net external force acts.

**Answer: Total momentum before collision = total momentum after collision.**

# Question 14. Two blocks released by cutting a cord

Given: m_A = 3.0 kg, m_B = 1.5 kg, u_A = u_B = 0, and v_B = +6.0 m/s (right).

Initial total momentum is zero. By conservation: m_A v_A + m_B v_B = 0.
(3.0)v_A + (1.5)(6.0) = 0 → 3v_A = −9 → v_A = −3.0 m/s.

**Answer: Block A moves at 3.0 m/s to the left.**

# Question 15. Two cars stick together after collision

> [!NOTE]
> 
> Given: m₁ = 1000 kg, u₁ = 15 m/s; m₂ = 1500 kg, u₂ = 0.
> 
> Common velocity: v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁ + m₂) = (1000×15 + 1500×0)/2500 = 6.0 m/s.
> 
> Initial KE = ½(1000)(15²) = 112,500 J.
> 
> Final KE = ½(2500)(6²) = 45,000 J.
> 
> KE lost = 112,500 − 45,000 = 67,500 J.
> 
> **Answer: Common velocity = 6.0 m/s; kinetic energy lost = 67,500 J.**

# Question 16. Elastic head-on collision of two balls

> [!NOTE]
> Given: m₁ = 0.40 kg, u₁ = 6.0 m/s; m₂ = 0.60 kg, u₂ = 0.
> 
> For a 1D elastic collision:
> 
> v₁ = [(m₁ − m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂] / (m₁ + m₂)
> 
> v₂ = [2m₁u₁ + (m₂ − m₁)u₂] / (m₁ + m₂)
> 
> Substitute u₂ = 0: v₁ = [(0.40 − 0.60)(6.0)]/1.00 = −1.2 m/s.
> 
> v₂ = [2(0.40)(6.0)]/1.00 = 4.8 m/s.
> 
> KE before = ½(0.40)(6.0²) = 7.2 J.
> 
> KE after = ½(0.40)(1.2²) + ½(0.60)(4.8²) = 0.288 + 6.912 = 7.2 J.**Answer: Ball 1: 1.2 m/s backwards; Ball 2: 4.8 m/s forwards. KE is conserved (7.2 J before and after).**

# Question 17. Bullet embeds in a block

> [!NOTE]

> 
> They stick together, so v = (m_bullet u_bullet)/(m_bullet + m_block) = (0.010×400)/(2.00) = 2.0 m/s.

> 
> Final KE = ½(2.00)(2.0²) = 4.0 J.

> **Answer: Speed = 2.0 m/s; kinetic energy lost = 796 J.**

# Question 18. Force of 3000 N at 60° above the ground

> [!NOTE]
> 
> Assume the force is directed 60° above the horizontal as described.
> 
> Horizontal component: Fₓ = F cosθ = 3000 cos60° = 1500 N.
> 
> Vertical component: Fᵧ = F sinθ = 3000 sin60° ≈ 2598 N upward.
> 
> Resultant magnitude: F = √(Fₓ² + Fᵧ²) = √(1500² + 2598²) ≈ 3000 N.
> 
> **Answer: Fₓ = 1500 N; Fᵧ ≈ 2598 N upward; resultant = 3000 N. (If the figure shows a downward push, the vertical component is downward instead.)**
>

# Question 21. Work done by a constant force

> [!NOTE]
> 
> Initial position = (−2, 5) m; final position = (4, 3) m.
> 
> Displacement: Δr = (4 − (−2), 3 − 5) = (6, −2) m.
> 
> Force F = (4, 3) N. Work W = F · Δr = (4)(6) + (3)(−2) = 24 − 6 = 18 J.
> 
> **Answer: Work done = 18 J.**

# Question 29. Centre of mass: equal masses on right triangle

> [!NOTE]
> Using the stated coordinates: A = (0, 0), B = (2.00, 0), C = (2.00, 1.50) m. All masses are 2.50 kg.
> 
> For equal masses, centre of mass is the average of the coordinates.
> 
> x_cm = (0 + 2.00 + 2.00)/3 = 1.333 m.
> 
> y_cm = (0 + 0 + 1.50)/3 = 0.500 m.
> 
> **Answer: Centre of mass = (1.33 m, 0.50 m).**

# Question 30. Centre of mass: unequal masses

> [!NOTE]
> Coordinates: A = (0, 0), B = (2.00, 0), C = (2.00, 1.50) m; m_A = 1.0 kg, m_B = 2.0 kg, m_C = 3.0 kg.
> 
> Total mass M = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 kg.
> 
> x_cm = (m_Ax_A + m_Bx_B + m_Cx_C)/M = [1(0) + 2(2.00) + 3(2.00)]/6 = 10/6 = 1.667 m.
> 
> y_cm = (m_Ay_A + m_By_B + m_Cy_C)/M = [1(0) + 2(0) + 3(1.50)]/6 = 4.5/6 = 0.750 m.
> 
> **Answer: Centre of mass = (1.67 m, 0.75 m).**
