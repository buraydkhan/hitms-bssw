# WEEK 5

## Islamic Law & Jurisprudence

### Key Terminology

> [!NOTE]
> - **Shariah:** The divine, all-encompassing guidance and rules revealed by Allah.
>     
> - **Fiqh:** Human understanding, extraction, and application of Islamic legal rulings from primary sources.
>     
> - **Usul al-Fiqh:** The principles and methodology of Islamic jurisprudence used to derive laws.
>     
> - **Hukm Shar'i:** A divine legal ruling/value attached to human actions (e.g., obligatory, recommended, permissible, disliked, forbidden).
>     
> - **Mujtahid:** A qualified Islamic scholar who exercises independent legal reasoning (_Ijtihad_) to derive rulings.
>     
> - **Islamic Law vs. Fiqh:** **Islamic Law (Shariah)** is divine, fixed, and eternal, whereas **Fiqh** is human interpretation, dynamic, and subject to evolution.
>     

### Legal Foundations & Sources

> [!NOTE]
> - **Return Disputes to Revelation:** Core Quranic directive requiring legal matters and disputes to be referred back to Allah and His Messenger (_Surah An-Nisa 4:59_).
>     
> - **Quranic Legal Foundations:** Key principles established in the Quran covering:
>     
>     - Justice
>         
>     - Contracts & Agreements
>         
>     - Prohibition of Usury (_Riba_)
>         
>     - Protection of Life
>         
>     - Purity & Cleanliness
>         

### Primary Secondary Sources: Ijma & Qiyas

#### **Ijma (Consensus)**

> [!NOTE]
> - **Definition:** Unanimous agreement of qualified Muslim jurists (_Mujtahids_) on a legal issue after the death of the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ).
>     
> - **Key Conditions:** Requires consensus of recognized scholars during a specific era on a matter of law.
>     
> - **Quranic Indication:** Grounded in scriptural directives to follow the believers' path and maintain unity.
>     
> - **Result:** Produces binding, definitive legal rulings.
>     

#### **Qiyas (Analogical Reasoning)**

- **Definition:** Extending an established rule from an existing case to a new case due to a shared underlying cause (_'Illah_).
    
- **4 Pillars of Qiyas:**
    
    1. **Asl (Original Case):** The primary case established in scripture.
        
    2. **Far' (New Case):** The new situation requiring a ruling.
        
    3. **'Illah (Effective Cause):** The common underlying attribute/reason shared by both cases.
        
    4. **Hukm (Ruling):** The established legal ruling applied from the _Asl_ to the _Far'_.
        
- **Result:** Allows Islamic law to adapt and address novel contemporary issues.
    

### Historical Development & Ikhtilaf

#### **Historical Eras of Fiqh:**

- **610–632 CE:** Era of Prophetic Revelation.
    
- **632–661 CE:** Era of the Rightly Guided Caliphs (_Khulafa-e-Rashideen_).
    
- **661–750 CE:** Umayyad Period (emergence of distinct legal centers).
    
- **750–900 CE:** Abbasid Period (Formative Golden Age & systematization of the major legal schools/Madhabs).
    
- **900+ CE:** Period of Consolidation, Codification, and _Taqlid_.
    
- **Modern Era:** Contemporary reforms, institutional _Ijtihad_, and adapting to modern contexts.
    

#### **Nature of Ikhtilaf (Scholarly Differences):**

Differences among scholars typically stem from:

1. **Textual Interpretation:** Divergent understandings of linguistic ambiguity or scope.
    
2. **Hadith Assessment:** Varied criteria in evaluating the authenticity or application of prophetic narrations.
    
3. **Usul / Methodology:** Differences in legal methodology and prioritization of secondary sources.
    

### Maqasid al-Shariah (Objectives of Islamic Law)

The broader essential goals that Islamic rulings aim to preserve and protect:

1. **Din:** Protection of Faith/Religion.
    
2. **Nafs:** Protection of Human Life.
    
3. **'Aql:** Protection of Intellect/Mind.
    
4. **Nasl:** Protection of Lineage/Family.
    
5. **Mal:** Protection of Wealth/Property.
    

- **Application:** Used by jurists to ensure legal decisions preserve public interest (_Maslahah_) and prevent harm.
