## Stages of Preservation & Codification

> [!NOTE] Stages
> 1. **Prophetic Era (610–632 CE)**
>     
>     - **Oral**: Memorized by Sahabah; annual Ramadan review with Angel Jibril AS (_Bukhari 4998_).
>         
>     - **Written**: Recorded on parchments, stones, and leather by scribes like Zayd bin Thabit RA (scattered status).
>         
> 2. **Caliphate of Abu Bakr RA (632–634 CE) — First Compilation**
>     
>     - **Cause**: ~70 _Huffaz_ martyred at Battle of Yamama.
>         
>     - **Execution**: Zayd bin Thabit RA bound scattered media into one master volume (_Suhuf_) using strict 2-witness verification (_Bukhari 4986_).
>         
> 3. **Caliphate of Uthman RA (644–656 CE) — Standardization**
>     
>     - **Cause**: Regional dialect disputes during military expansion (_Bukhari 4987_).
>         
>     - **Execution**: Master copy retrieved from Hafsah RA; copied in the **Quraishi dialect** (_Mushaf-e-Uthmani_), sent to major provinces, and private copies destroyed.
>         
> 4. **Post-Caliphate Script Evolution**
>     
>     - **Dotting (_I'jam_)**: Added by Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali (d. 688 CE) to distinguish letters ($ب, ت, ث$).
>         
>     - **Diacritics (_Tashkeel_)**: Added by Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi (d. 786 CE) for short vowel marks (_Fatha, Kasra, Damma_).
> 

### Abu Bakr (RA) vs. Uthman (RA) Compilations

| **Aspect**  | **Abu Bakr (RA)**                         | **Uthman (RA)**                                |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Trigger** | Loss of _Huffaz_ at Yamama                | Dialect conflicts in expanded empire           |
| **Goal**    | **Gather** scattered text into one volume | **Standardize** to a single dialect (Quraish)  |
| **Result**  | One central master manuscript (_Suhuf_)   | Official codices (_Masahif_) sent to provinces |

# WEEK 4

## Introduction to Hadith and Sunnah

> [!question] What is a Hadith and Sunnah ?
> **Hadith** refers to the recorded reports of the sayings, actions, approvals, and descriptions of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, transmitted by his companions. **Sunnah** refers to the established way, practice, or habitual example of the Prophet, which serves as a model for Muslim behavior

## Basic Concepts

> [!NOTE] Terminologies
> - **Sunnah**: Literally means "a path" or "a way of life." In Islamic legal theory (_Usul al-Fiqh_), it denotes the Prophet's words, actions, tacit approvals, and moral/physical traits.
>     
> - **Hadith**: Literally means "speech," "news," or "report." It is the formal text or tradition recording a specific instance of the Sunnah.
>     
> - **Sanad**: The chain of narrators through which a report is transmitted, linking the final compiler back to the Prophet.
>     
> - **Isnad**: The process of documenting, citing, or attesting the transmission chain (often used interchangeably with _sanad_).
>     
> - **Matn**: The actual textual content or message of the Hadith.
>     
> - **Rawi**: An individual narrator within the transmission chain.
>     
> - **Riwayah**: The formal discipline and practice of transmitting and preserving Hadith reports.

## Hadith Content

> [!NOTE] Categorized by the nature of their content into four major types
> 
> 1. **Qawli (Verbal Statements)**: Direct spoken words of the Prophet ﷺ.
>     
>     - _Example_: "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."
>         
> 2. **Fi'li (Actions & Practices)**: Descriptions of how the Prophet ﷺ performed deeds.
>     
>     - _Example_: Physical descriptions of how he performed ablution (_Wudu_) or Prayer (_Salah_).
>         
> 3. **Taqriri (Tacit Approvals)**: Actions performed by Companions in his presence (or known to him) that he did not object to or correct.
>     
>     - _Example_: The eating of a lizard (_Dabb_) at a meal where the Prophet was present; he refrained from eating it himself but did not forbid others.
>         
> 4. **Qudsi (Sacred Reports)**: Narrations where the Prophet quotes Allah directly in the first person ("Allah says..."), but the wording is expressed in the Prophet's own words.
>     
>     - _Distinction from the Qur'an_: Qur'anic revelation is divine in both meaning and precise wording, recited in ritual prayer, and inimitable; Hadith Qudsi is divine in meaning only and is not recited in prayer.

## Kinds of Hadith

> [!NOTE] Classification
> 
> Hadith are classified across three main dimensions: transmission volume, authenticity, and ultimate endpoint.
> 
> ### A. By Number of Narrators (Reach)
> 
> - **Mutawatir (Mass-Transmitted)**: Narrated by such a large number of independent chains at every level of transmission that collusion on a lie or error is impossible. Yields absolute, certain knowledge (_Yaqin_).
>     
> - **Ahad (Solitary / Non-Mutawatir)**: Reports transmitted through a limited number of chains. Subdivided into:
>     
>     - **Mashhur (Famous)**: Reported by 3 or more narrators at every level of the chain, but falling short of Mutawatir.
>         
>     - **'Aziz (Scarce/Strong)**: Reported by at least 2 narrators at every tier of the chain.
>         
>     - **Gharib (Solitary)**: Reported by only a single narrator at some point in the chain.
>         
> 
> ### B. By Grade of Authenticity
> 
> - **Sahih (Authentic)**: Fulfills all five criteria of authenticity perfectly.
>     
> - **Hasan (Good/Fair)**: Fulfills all criteria of Sahih, except that one or more narrators have slightly lesser memory precision (_dabt_). Legally binding.
>     
> - **Da'if (Weak)**: Fails to satisfy one or more conditions of Sahih/Hasan (e.g., a broken chain or a narrator with poor memory).
>     
> - **Mawdu' (Fabricated)**: False reports falsely invented and attributed to the Prophet; rejected completely.
>     
> 
> ### C. By Attribution Endpoint
> 
> - **Marfu' (Elevated)**: Traced directly to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.
>     
> - **Mawquf (Stopped)**: Traced to a Companion (_Sahabi_) only.
>     
> - **Maqtu' (Severed)**: Traced to a Successor (_Taabi'i_) or subsequent generation.

## Anatomy of Hadith

Every complete Hadith consists of two primary components: the **Sanad** (chain) and the **Matn** (text).

```
[SANAD / CHAIN OF TRANSMISSION]                           [MATN / TEXT]
Narrator A  ──>  Narrator B  ──>  Narrator C  ──>  Prophet ﷺ  ──> "Actions are judged by intentions..."
(Compiler)       (Successor)     (Companion)
```

> [!question] Example
> > _Al-Bukhari narrated to us, saying: Yahya ibn Sa'id reported to us, from Muhammad ibn Ibrahim, from 'Alqamah ibn Waqqas, who heard 'Umar ibn al-Khattab say on the pulpit:_ **"I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: 'Actions are judged by intentions...'"**
> > 
> > - **Sanad**: Al-Bukhari $\rightarrow$ Yahya $\rightarrow$ Muhammad $\rightarrow$ 'Alqamah $\rightarrow$ 'Umar.
> >     
> > - **Matn**: "Actions are judged by intentions..."

## Uloom ul-Hadith & Historical Compilation

**'Uloom ul-Hadith** (Sciences of Hadith) encompasses sub-disciplines developed to evaluate narrations, including **'Ilm al-Rijal** (Biographical Evaluation of Transmitters) and **Al-Jarh wa al-Ta'dil** (Critique of Narrator Trustworthiness).

## Eras of Codification

1. **Prophetic & Companion Era (1st Century AH)**: Primary preservation was oral, alongside personal written sheets (_Suhuf_), such as the _Sahifah al-Sadiqah_ compiled by 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As.
    
2. **Official Systematic Codification (2nd Century AH)**: Caliph 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz commissioned formal gathering. Landmark collections emerged combining Hadith and legal opinions, notably Imam Malik's _Al-Muwatta_.
    
3. **The Golden Era of Hadith Science (3rd Century AH)**: Scholars developed rigorous critical methods, separating pure Sahih Hadith from weaker traditions.

## Legal Position & Authority of the Sunnah

> [!NOTE] Explanation
> In Islamic jurisprudence (_Usul al-Fiqh_), the Sunnah is the **second primary source of divine law**, authoritative (_Hujjah_) by Qur'anic mandate (e.g., _"Obey Allah and obey the Messenger"_, Surah An-Nisa 4:59).
> 
> The Sunnah functions in relation to the Qur'an in four main ways:
> 
> 1. **Confirmation (_Ta'keed_)**: Reaffirming rulings explicitly stated in the Qur'an (e.g., the obligation of prayer, prohibition of theft).
>     
> 2. **Clarification & Details (_Bayan_)**: Explaining general or concise Qur'anic commands. The Qur'an commands prayer (_Salah_) and almsgiving (_Zakat_), but the Sunnah details the exact prayer postures, times, and financial thresholds (_Nisab_).
>     
> 3. **Qualification & Restriction (_Takhsis & Taqyid_)**: Specifying general Qur'anic rules or restricting unqualified statements.
>     
> 4. **Independent Legislation (_Insha'_)**: Establishing rulings on matters not explicitly detailed in the Qur'an (e.g., prohibition of men wearing gold and pure silk, or prohibiting the consumption of beasts of prey).

## What Makes a Hadith Sahih (Authentic)?

For a Hadith to be graded as **Sahih** (authentic and legally binding), Hadith critics require it to fulfill **five strict criteria**:

| **Condition**                      | **Arabic Term**     | **Definition**                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Continuous Chain**            | _Ittisal al-Sanad_  | Every narrator in the chain heard the report directly from their immediate teacher without gaps or omitted links.                      |
| **2. Moral Integrity**             | _'Adalah al-Rawi_   | Every narrator must be a pious, honest, and upright Muslim, free from major sins, public immorality, or deceptive behavior.            |
| **3. Precision & Retention**       | _Dabt_              | Every narrator must possess precise retention—either by memory (_dabt al-sadr_) or through verified written records (_dabt al-kitab_). |
| **4. Freedom from Irregularity**   | _'Adam al-Shudhudh_ | The report must not contradict another narration transmitted by a more trustworthy or authoritative narrator.                          |
| **5. Freedom from Hidden Defects** | _'Adam al-'Illah_   | The report must be free from subtle, hidden flaws ('_illah_) that undermine authenticity despite an apparently sound chain.            |
