# WEEK 1

## UNDERSTANDING THE BASIC CONCEPT OF RELIGION 

> [!NOTE] DEEN AND RELIGION FROM A ISLAMIC POV
> - **Religion (Madhhab / Specialized Practice):** In Western discourse, "religion" often refers to personal faith, rituals, and acts of worship (like prayer and fasting) separate from public or civic life.
> - **Deen (Complete Way of Life):** _Deen_ encompasses all aspects of existence. It includes theology (Akida), acts of worship (Ibadah), ethics (Akhlaq), laws (Sharia), business conduct (Muamalat), and social governance.

> [!question] What is Religion?
>  Religion is a system of beliefs and practices that connect human beings with God and provides guidance for life.
> Provides a framework for understanding God, life and human responsibilites. Guidance for individual and social behaviour. Gives directions for moral and spiritual.

> [!NOTE] What it gives you :
> LIfe -> Purpose and meaning
> Moral guidance -> right and wrong 
> Spiritual -> Satisfaction and hope
> Guidance -> personal and social life
> Sense of accountibility

## Major World Religions

- Islam, Judaism, Christainity, Hinduism

> [!question] Foundational Framework
> Human Diversity & Original Monotheism in Quran
> 
> 1. Universal Diversity as Divine Design
> - **Natural and Human Variety:** Differences in human languages, skin colors, cultures, and the natural world are explicitly framed as "signs" (_ayat_) of Divine wisdom for those who reflect.
> - **Refusal of Forced Uniformity:** The Qur'an states that God could have made humanity a single, uniform nation, but chose varied identities so people would learn to interact across differences rather than retreat into tribalism.
> 2. Intentional Religious Pluralism
> - **Multiple Paths and Laws:** The text asserts that different communities received distinct laws and spiritual paths. This variation is described as a purposeful test of faith and righteousness across distinct traditions.
> - **The Cosmic Purpose:** The primary goal of this pluralistic structure is to encourage communities to **"compete in doing good"** (_fastabiqu'l-khayrat_), using moral action rather than doctrinal dominance as the ultimate measure.
> 3. Divine Intent: Recognition, Not Division
> - **"To Know One Another":** Humans are divided into nations and tribes specifically so they may seek mutual understanding (_li-ta'arafu_), establishing inter-group knowledge and respect as a cosmic mandate.
> - **Moral Standing Over Identity:** In the Qur'anic framework, universal human dignity precedes group affiliation; individual merit and standing before God are judged solely by moral consciousness and right action (_taqwa_).
> 

## JUDAISM 

> [!NOTE] Explanation
> Core Belief & Theology
> Strict Monotheism -> Belief in Yahweh as the sole Creator and Sovereign of the universe. 
> Covenant: Sacred alliance between God and the Children of Israel established through Abraham and Moses.
> Scripture: Tanakh comprising Torah (Law), Nevi'im (Prophets), and Ketuvim (Writings).
> 
> Beliefs & Practices    
> Sabbath (Shabbat/سَبْت  السَّبْت  شَبَّت): Weekly day of rest and spiritual devotion from Friday sunset to Saturday night.
> ashrut (Kosher): Strict dietary laws governing permissible and forbidden foods.
> Daily Prayer: Thrice daily liturgical recitations including the Shema Yisrael
> 
> 
> Status of Torah & People of the Book
> The Quran acknowledges the Torah (Tawrat) as divine revelation granted to Prophet Musa (A.S), containing guidance and light for humanity.Honor given to Biblical Prophets (Musa, Harun, Dawud)Critique of historical covenant breaches and alterations.Recognition of righteous scholars among them."It was We who revealed the law (to Moses): therein was guidance and light..." (Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:44)

## CHRISTAINITY

> [!NOTE] Explanation
> Sacraments: Sacred rites believed to be channels of divine grace.
> Baptism: Initiation into the Christian faith through water, symbolizing purification, death and rebirth in Christ. Usually done in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
> Eucharist (Holy Communion / Lord's Supper): Central act of worship commemorating Jesus' Last Supper. Bread and wine are shared as the body and blood of Christ.
> Scripture: The Bible Composed of two parts: 
> Old Testament: Shared with Judaism - Law, Prophets, Writings, history of covenant with Israel.
> New Testament: 27 books about life and teachings of Jesus Christ (4 Gospels), Acts of Apostles, Epistles (letters), and Revelation.Christians believe the Bible is the inspired Word of God and ultimate authority for faith and practice.
> 
> 
> Jesus in the Quran Prophet Isa (A.S) & Maryam 
> Islam highly venerates Jesus as a major Prophet and Messianic figure born miraculously to the Virgin Mary (Maryam A.S).The Quran firmly rejects the Trinity and divine sonship, affirming strict monotheism while honoring Jesus' miraculous works."Christ the son of Mary was no more than a messenger; many were the messengers that passed away before him..." (Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:75)


## HINDUISM

> [!NOTE] Explanation
> Brahman: The ultimate, supreme reality - formless, infinite, eternal source of all existence. All gods and goddesses are manifestations of Brahman.
> Karma: Law of cause and effect. Every action has consequences that affect one's future, in this life and the next.
> Reincarnation (Samsara): The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth. The soul (Atman) transmigrates based on Karma until liberation is achieved.Moksha: Ultimate goal of life - liberation from Samsara and union with Brahman. Freedom from the cycle of suffering.
> Sacred Texts
> Shruti (Revealed): Vedas (Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva), Upanishads - philosophical teachings about Brahman and Atman.
> Smriti (Remembered): Bhagavad Gita, Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas - epics, stories and laws for daily life.
> Practices & WorshipPuja: 
> Daily worship/offering to deities at home or in Mandir (temple)
> Yoga & Meditation: 
> Disciplines to control mind and realize union with Brahman
> Dharma: Righteous duty and moral living
> Pilgrimage & Festivals: Kumbh Mela, Diwali, Holi etc.
