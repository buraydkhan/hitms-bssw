# WEEK 3

## POSITION & DISPLACEMENT

> [!NOTE] Definition
> - **Position ($x$):** The location of an object relative to a chosen reference point 
> - **Displacement ($\Delta x$):** The straight-line change in position. It is a **vector** quantity, meaning direction matters (positive or negative).    $$\Delta x = x_f - x_i$$
>     _(Where $x_f$ is final position and $x_i$ is initial position)_

### DIsplacement & Distance

- **Distance ($d$):** The total path length traveled. It is a **scalar** quantity (always positive or zero).
- Displacement depends only on the endpoints, whereas distance depends on the complete path taken.

- **Displacement is not always equal to the  distance traveled! • Displacement can be positive or negative**

- **POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE QUANTITIES ARE JUST DIRECTIONS IN MOTION PROBLEMS!**

## Velocity vs. Speed

> [!NOTE] Avg Velocity and Speed
> - **Average Velocity ($v_{\text{avg}}$):** The rate of change of displacement over time. It is a **vector** (has direction).
>     $$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{x_f - x_i}{t_f - t_i}$$
> - **Average Speed ($s_{\text{avg}}$):** The total path distance traveled divided by total time elapsed. It is a **scalar**.
>     $$s_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\Delta t}$$
> 
> 

### Acceleration

> [!NOTE] Avg Acceleration
> 
> - **Average Acceleration ($a_{\text{avg}}$):** The rate at which velocity changes over time. It is a **vector**, measured in meters per second squared ($\text{m/s}^2$).
>     
>     $$a_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - v_i}{t_f - t_i}$$

> [!NOTE] Relating Velocity and Acceleration
> The relationship between the directions of velocity ($v$) and acceleration ($a$) dictates whether an object speeds up or slows down:
> 
> - **Speeding Up:** $v$ and $a$ have the **same sign** (both positive or both negative).
>     
> - **Slowing Down (Deceleration):** $v$ and $a$ have **opposite signs** (one positive, one negative).
>     
> - **Constant Speed:** $a = 0$. Velocity remains unchanged.

## Graphical Representation

> [!NOTE] Graphical Representation of Motion
> ### Position vs. Time Graph ($x$ vs. $t$)
> 
> - **Slope:** Represents the **velocity** of the object ($\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = v$).
>     
> - **Straight line:** Constant velocity.
>     
> - **Curved line:** Changing velocity (acceleration is present).
>     
> - **Flat horizontal line:** Object is at rest ($v = 0$).
>     

> [!NOTE] Graph
> ### Velocity vs. Time Graph ($v$ vs. $t$)
> 
> - **Slope:** Represents the **acceleration** ($\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = a$).
>     
> - **Area under the curve:** Represents the **displacement** ($\Delta x$).
>     
> - **Flat horizontal line:** Constant velocity ($a = 0$).
>     

## Motion with Constant Acceleration

> [!NOTE] Explanation
> When acceleration remains uniform ($a = \text{constant}$), the fundamental equations of motion apply:
> 
> 1. **Velocity-Time Equation:**
>     
>     $$v_f = v_i + a t$$
>     
> 2. **Position-Time Equation:**
>     
>     $$\Delta x = v_i t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$
>     
> 1. **Timeless Equation:**
>     
>     $$v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2 a \Delta x$$
>     
> 2. **Average Velocity Equation:**
>     
>     $$\Delta x = \left(\frac{v_i + v_f}{2}\right) t$$
>     
> 
> Constant Acceleration Starting from Rest ($v_i = 0$)
> 
> When an object accelerates starting from a complete standstill, the standard kinematic equations simplify as follows:
> 
> - **Final Speed:**
>     
>     $$v_f = a t$$
>     
> - **Average Speed / Average Velocity:**
>     
>     $$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{0 + v_f}{2} = \frac{v_f}{2} = \frac{1}{2} a t$$
>     
> - **Distance Traveled:**
>     
>     $$d = \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$
>     
> 

## Free Fall Motion

> [!NOTE] Explanaion
> Free fall is 1D vertical motion where air resistance is neglected and the only acting force is gravity. Acceleration is constant at $g \approx 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ (directed downwards).
> 
> ### Dropped from Rest ($v_i = 0$, downward taken as positive):
> 
> - **Impact Speed ($v_{\text{impact}}$):** The speed right before striking the ground after falling from height $h$.
>     
>     $$v_{\text{impact}} = \sqrt{2 g h}$$
>     
> - **Fall Time ($t_{\text{fall}}$):** The duration required to fall from height $h$.
>     
>     $$t_{\text{fall}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 h}{g}}$$
>     
>

## Momentum 

> [!NOTE]
> ### 1. Linear Momentum ($\vec{p}$)
> 
> - **Concept:** A measure of a moving body's mass and velocity (vector quantity).
>     
> - **Formula:** $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$
>     
> - **Units:** $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ or $\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$
>     
> 
> ### 2. Momentum & Newton's Second Law
> 
> - **Concept:** The net external force acting on an object equals the rate of change of its linear momentum.
>     
> - **Formulas:**
>     
>     $$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$$
>     
>     _For constant mass:_ $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = m\frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t} = m\vec{a}$
>     
> 
> ### 3. Impulse ($\vec{J}$)
> 
> - **Concept:** The overall effect of a force acting over a time interval; equals the change in linear momentum (**Impulse-Momentum Theorem**).
>     
> - **Formulas:**
>     
>     $$\vec{J} = \int \vec{F} \, dt = \vec{F}_{\text{avg}} \Delta t = \Delta \vec{p} = \vec{p}_f - \vec{p}_i$$
>     
> 

## 2. Conservation of Linear Momentum

> [!NOTE] Explanation
> - **Concept:** The total linear momentum of an isolated system ($\vec{F}_{\text{ext, net}} = 0$) remains constant over time.
>     
> - **Formula:**
>     
>     $$\sum \vec{p}_i = \sum \vec{p}_f \implies m_1\vec{v}_{1i} + m_2\vec{v}_{2i} = m_1\vec{v}_{1f} + m_2\vec{v}_{2f}$$
>     

## 3. Elastic vs. Inelastic Collisions

> [!NOTE]
> #### **Elastic Collision**
> 
> - **Concept:** Both momentum and kinetic energy ($K$) are conserved (no loss to heat/sound).
>     
> - **Formulas:**
>     
>     1. Momentum: $m_1 v_{1i} + m_2 v_{2i} = m_1 v_{1f} + m_2 v_{2f}$
>         
>     2. Kinetic Energy: $\frac{1}{2}m_1 v_{1i}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_{2i}^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_{1f}^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_{2f}^2$
>         
>     3. Relative Velocity relation: $(v_{1i} - v_{2i}) = -(v_{1f} - v_{2f})$
>         
> 
> #### **Inelastic Collision**
> 
> - **Concept:** Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is **not** conserved ($K$ is lost).
>     
>     - **Completely Inelastic Collision:** The objects stick together post-collision ($v_{1f} = v_{2f} = v_f$).
>         
> - **Formula (Completely Inelastic):**
>     
>     $$m_1 v_{1i} + m_2 v_{2i} = (m_1 + m_2)v_f$$
>     
> 

## 4. Center of Mass ($R_{\text{cm}}$)

> [!NOTE]
> - **Concept:** The unique point where the total mass of a system can be treated as concentrated for translational motion.
>     
> - **Formulas:**
>     
>     - **Position:**
>         
>         $$\vec{R}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{r}_i}{M_{\text{total}}} = \frac{m_1\vec{r}_1 + m_2\vec{r}_2 + \dots}{m_1 + m_2 + \dots}$$
>         
>     - **Velocity:**
>         
>         $$\vec{V}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum m_i \vec{v}_i}{M_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\vec{P}_{\text{total}}}{M_{\text{total}}}$$
>         
>     - **Acceleration:**
>         
>         $$\vec{A}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{\sum \vec{F}_{\text{ext}}}{M_{\text{total}}}$$
