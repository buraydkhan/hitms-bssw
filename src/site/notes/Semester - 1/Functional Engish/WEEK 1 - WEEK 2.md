# WEEK 1

Senteces -> Building Blocks = Words <- Letters <- Phonics

> [!NOTE] Phrases and its types :
> A **phrase** is a group of two or more words that work together as a single grammatical unit within a sentence. A phrase lacks both a subject and a verb working together, so it cannot express a complete thought on its own.
> 
> ### Key Types of Phrases
> 
> - **Noun Phrase (NP):** Consists of a noun and its modifiers; functions as a subject, object, or complement in a sentence.
>     
>     - _Example:_ **The high-speed fiber connection** dropped suddenly.
>         
> - **Verb Phrase (VP):** Consists of a main verb along with its auxiliary (helping) verbs.
>     
>     - _Example:_ System logs **have been updating** continuously.
>         
> - **Prepositional Phrase (PP):** Begins with a preposition and ends with a noun/pronoun object; acts as an adjective or adverb.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The database server is located **in the primary data center**.
>         
> - **Adjective Phrase (AdjP):** A group of words headed by an adjective that modifies a noun or pronoun.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The user interface was **surprisingly easy to configure**.
>         
> - **Adverb Phrase (AdvP):** A group of words headed by an adverb that modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The script executed the automated test **much faster than expected**.

> [!question] What is Sentence ?
> A grammatically complete group of words containing at least one subject and a predicate (verb) that expresses a complete thought, statement, question, command, or exclamation.
> 

> [!question] What is a Clause ?
>
> 
> A **clause** is a building block of a sentence containing both a **subject** and a **predicate**.
> 
> - **Independent (Main) Clause:** Expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a simple sentence.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The system booted up.
>         
> - **Dependent (Subordinate) Clause:** Contains a subject and verb but starts with a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun, making it an incomplete thought that relies on an independent clause.
>     
>     - _Example:_ **Because the connection timed out**, the script failed.
>         
> 

> [!NOTE] Types of Sentences :
> Sentences are categorized by structural complexity and by functional intent.
> 
> #### By Structure
> 
> - **Simple Sentence:** Contains one independent clause and no dependent clauses.
>     
>     - _Example:_ Python executes code line by line.
>         
> - **Compound Sentence:** Contains two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) or a semicolon.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The server crashed, **but** the backup remained intact.
>         
> - **Complex Sentence:** Contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.
>     
>     - _Example:_ **Although the API failed**, the application rendered cached data.
>         
> - **Compound-Complex Sentence:** Contains at least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause.
>     
>     - _Example:_ **When the user submitted the form**, the database saved the record, **and** the client redirected to home.
>         
> 
> #### By Purpose / Function
> 
> - **Declarative:** Makes a statement of fact or opinion; ends with a period (`.`).
>     
> - **Interrogative:** Asks a direct question; ends with a question mark (`?`).
>     
> - **Imperative:** Gives a command, instruction, or request; ends with a period or exclamation mark (`.` / `!`).
>     
> - **Exclamatory:** Expresses strong emotion or surprise; ends with an exclamation mark (`!`).
>     

> [!NOTE] Adjective-Only Sentences :
> 
> 
> In standard English grammar, a complete sentence **must contain a verb**. True "adjective-only" sentences do not exist in standard writing, but adjectives can form complete utterances in two specific contexts:
> 
> - **Informal Minor Sentences (Ellipsis):** Adjectives used as single-word conversational answers or exclamations where the subject and verb are implied by context.
>     
>     - _Example:_ "How is the code?" $\rightarrow$ **"Efficient."** (Implies: _"The code is efficient."_)
>         
>     - _Example:_ **"Unbelievable!"** (Implies: _"This is unbelievable!"_)
>         
> - **Adjectives with Linking Verbs (Predicate Adjectives):** The closest standard grammatical equivalent where the sentence consists of a subject, a linking verb, and an adjective complement.
>     
>     - _Example:_ **"It is fast."** or **"Data remains secure."**

> [!NOTE] > PARTS OF SPEECH :
> - **Noun:** Names a person, place, thing, concept, or idea (_Linux, student, execution_)
> 	- - **Concrete Noun:** Things that physically exist and can be perceived by the 5 senses.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ keyboard, paper, processor.
>         
> 	- **Abstract Noun:** Intangible concepts, states, qualities, or ideas that cannot be physically touched.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ efficiency, logic, faith, speed.
>       
> 	- **Collective Noun:** A singular word used to represent a collection or group of individuals as a single unit.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ team, committee, flock, array.
>         
> 	- **Group Noun:** Frequently used interchangeably with collective nouns; specifically highlights a set of distinct elements acting together or as individual members of a designated set.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ panel (of judges), cluster (of servers), family.
>         
> 	- **Material Noun:** Names of raw materials, substances, or ingredients from which things are made.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ silicon, copper, gold, water.
>         
> 	- **Countable Noun:** Items that can be counted individually using numbers.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ file, loop, error, module.
>         
> 	- **Uncountable (Mass) Noun:** Substances or concepts that cannot be counted directly without units of measurement.
>     
>     - _Examples:_ bandwidth, memory, code, information.
>     
> - **Pronoun:** Replaces a noun to avoid repetition (_he, it, which, static_).
>     
> - **Verb:** Asserts an action, event, or state of being (_compiles, runs, is, execute_).
>     
> - **Adjective:** Modifies or describes a noun or pronoun (_fast, modular, efficient_).
>     
> - **Adverb:** Modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb (_swiftly, very, programmatically_).
>     
> - **Preposition:** Shows temporal, spatial, or logical relationships between nouns (_in, at, under, through_).
>     
> - **Conjunction:** Connects words, phrases, or clauses (_and, but, because, although_).
>     
> - **Interjection:** Expresses sudden emotion or exclamation (_Oh, Hurrah, Alas_).

> [!NOTE] Auxiliary Noun :
> An **attributive noun**, this occurs when a noun acts in a supporting (auxiliary) role to modify another noun directly in front of it.
> - **Concept:** The first noun describes or specifies the second noun, functioning like an adjective.
> - **Examples:**
>     - **Computer** network _(computer is a noun modifying network)_
>     - **Data** pipeline _(data is a noun modifying pipeline)_
>     - **System** architecture _(system is a noun modifying architecture)_

# WEEK 2

> [!NOTE] Subject-Verb Agreement Rules
> The fundamental rule is that **a singular subject takes a singular verb**, and **a plural subject takes a plural verb**.
> 
> 1. **Intervening Phrases:** Words that come between the subject and the verb do not change the subject's number.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The **box** of heavy tools **is** in the closet. (_Box_ is singular, not _tools_).
>         
> 2. **Compound Subjects with "And":** Two or more subjects joined by _and_ generally take a plural verb.
>     
>     - _Example:_ **Python and C++** **are** powerful languages.
>         
> 3. **Compound Subjects with "Or" / "Nor":** The verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
>     
>     - _Example:_ Neither the supervisor nor the **engineers** **were** present.
>         
>     - _Example:_ Neither the engineers nor the **supervisor** **was** present.
>         
> 4. **Indefinite Pronouns:**
>     
>     - Always singular: _everyone, somebody, each, neither, either, anyone, nobody_.
>         
>     - Always plural: _both, few, many, several_.
>         
>     - _Example:_ **Each** of the applicants **has** submitted an application.
>         
>     - _Example:_ **Many** **have** completed the exam.
>         
> 5. **Collective Nouns:** Nouns like _team, committee, family, jury_ take a singular verb when acting as a unified group.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The **committee** **meets** every Tuesday.
>         
> 6. **Inverted Sentences (There / Here):** The verb agrees with the subject that follows it.
>     
>     - _Example:_ There **are** three **bugs** in this script.
>         
> 

> [!NOTE] Tenses and Aspects
> Tense indicates **time** (Past, Present, Future), while aspect indicates how the action unfolds in relation to time (**Simple, Continuous/Progressive, Perfect, Perfect Continuous**).
> 
> ### Present Tense
> 
> - **Simple Present:** General truths, habits, and facts.
>     
>     - _Example:_ She **writes** clean code.
>         
> - **Present Continuous:** Action happening right now or temporarily.
>     
>     - _Example:_ She **is writing** a new script.
>         
> - **Present Perfect:** Action completed at an unspecified time with relevance to the present.
>     
>     - _Example:_ She **has written** three chapters.
>         
> - **Present Perfect Continuous:** Action started in the past and continues into the present.
>     
>     - _Example:_ She **has been writing** since morning.
>         
> 
> ### Past Tense
> 
> - **Simple Past:** Action completed at a specific time in the past.
>     
>     - _Example:_ They **deployed** the update yesterday.
>         
> - **Past Continuous:** Action in progress at a specific moment in the past.
>     
>     - _Example:_ They **were deploying** the update when the server crashed.
>         
> - **Past Perfect:** Action completed before another past action occurred.
>     
>     - _Example:_ They **had deployed** the update before the outage happened.
>         
> - **Past Perfect Continuous:** Continuous action that took place before another past point in time.
>     
>     - _Example:_ They **had been deploying** updates for hours before noticing the error.
>         
> 
> ### Future Tense
> 
> - **Simple Future:** Prediction or action planned for the future.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The team **will release** the patch tomorrow.
>         
> - **Future Continuous:** Action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The team **will be releasing** the patch during the maintenance window.
>         
> - **Future Perfect:** Action that will be completed before a specified future time.
>     
>     - _Example:_ The team **will have released** the patch by midnight.
>         
> - **Future Perfect Continuous:** Action that will continue up until a specific point in the future.
>     
>     - _Example:_ By next month, the team **will have been releasing** patches for five years.
