# WEEK 3

## Rules of Ten Aspects System

> [!NOTE] 
> ## 1. Present Simple
> 
> - **Usage:** General truths, permanent states, habits, routines, and scheduled future events.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + Verb(s/es)`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + do/does + not + Base Verb`
>         
>     - Question: `Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Add `-s` or `-es` to the verb for third-person singular subjects (`he`, `she`, `it`).
>     
> - **Example:** _The sun rises in the east._ / _She works every weekday._
>     
> 
> ## 2. Present Continuous (Progressive)
> 
> - **Usage:** Actions happening right now, temporary situations, or definite future plans.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + am/is/are + not + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Question: `Am/Is/Are + Subject + Verb-ing?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Do not use with non-continuous/stative verbs (e.g., _know_, _like_, _believe_, _belong_).
>     
> - **Example:** _They are writing a report right now._ / _He is meeting us tomorrow._
>     
> 
> ## 3. Present Perfect
> 
> - **Usage:** Actions completed at an unspecified time before now, past events with present relevance, or life experiences.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + have/has + Past Participle (V3)`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + have/has + not + Past Participle (V3)`
>         
>     - Question: `Have/Has + Subject + Past Participle (V3)?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Use `has` for third-person singular (`he`, `she`, `it`) and `have` for all others. Do not specify exact past timestamps (use Present Perfect for "ever/never/already/yet").
>     
> - **Example:** _She has completed three modules today._
>     
> 
> ## 4. Present Perfect Continuous
> 
> - **Usage:** Actions that began in the past and are still continuing, or actions recently finished with visible present results.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + have/has + been + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + have/has + not + been + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Question: `Have/Has + Subject + been + Verb-ing?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Frequently paired with time expressions using **for** (duration) or **since** (starting point).
>     
> - **Example:** _We have been studying for four hours._
>     
> 
> ## 5. Past Simple
> 
> - **Usage:** Actions completed at a specific, finished time in the past.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + Verb-ed (V2)`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + did + not + Base Verb`
>         
>     - Question: `Did + Subject + Base Verb?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Regular verbs add `-ed`; irregular verbs change form completely (e.g., _go_ $\rightarrow$ _went_). In negative and question forms, the main verb returns to its base form because `did` carries the past tense.
>     
> - **Example:** _They submitted the final draft yesterday._
>     
> 
> ## 6. Past Continuous (Progressive)
> 
> - **Usage:** Ongoing actions in progress at a specific moment in the past, or an action interrupted by another past event.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + was/were + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + was/were + not + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Question: `Was/Were + Subject + Verb-ing?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Use `was` with singular subjects (`I`, `he`, `she`, `it`) and `were` with plural subjects (`we`, `you`, `they`). Often combined with Past Simple using _when_ or _while_.
>     
> - **Example:** _I was reading when the power went out._
>     
> 
> ## 7. Past Perfect
> 
> - **Usage:** An action that was completed before another action or time in the past (the "past before past").
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + had + Past Participle (V3)`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + had + not + Past Participle (V3)`
>         
>     - Question: `Had + Subject + Past Participle (V3)?`
>         
> - **Rules:** `had` stays identical regardless of the subject pronoun.
>     
> - **Example:** _The train had already left when we arrived at the station._
>     
> 
> ## 8. Past Perfect Continuous
> 
> - **Usage:** An ongoing action that continued up until another specific moment or event in the past.
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + had + been + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + had + not + been + Verb-ing`
>         
>     - Question: `Had + Subject + been + Verb-ing?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Emphasizes the duration of a past activity leading up to a past cutoff point.
>     
> - **Example:** _They had been negotiating for three weeks before signing the contract._
>     
> 
> ## 9. Future Simple & Aspectual Variations (Will / Be Going To)
> 
> - **Usage:** Predictions, spontaneous decisions (_will_), or planned future intentions (_be going to_).
>     
> - **Structure:**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + will + Base Verb` OR `Subject + am/is/are + going to + Base Verb`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + will + not (won't) + Base Verb`
>         
>     - Question: `Will + Subject + Base Verb?`
>         
> - **Rules:** `will` is followed directly by the base form without _to_.
>     
> - **Example:** _I will assist you with the analysis._ / _She is going to present tomorrow._
>     
> 
> ## 10. Future Perfect & Extended Aspects (Future In-the-Past)
> 
> - **Usage:** Actions that will be completed prior to a specific future moment, or actions viewed from a past perspective looking forward.
>     
> - **Structure (Future Perfect):**
>     
>     - Positive: `Subject + will + have + Past Participle (V3)`
>         
>     - Negative: `Subject + will + not + have + Past Participle (V3)`
>         
>     - Question: `Will + Subject + have + Past Participle (V3)?`
>         
> - **Rules:** Always use `have` after `will`, never `has`.
>     
> - **Example:** _By next month, the team will have finished the entire project._
>

## Active & Passive Voices

> [!NOTE]
> - **Active Voice:** The subject performs the action. It is direct, concise, and standard in everyday language.
>     
>     - _Structure:_ `Subject (Doer) + Verb + Object (Receiver)`
>         
>     - _Example:_ **The scribe** _wrote_ **the manuscript**.
>         
> - **Passive Voice:** The subject receives the action. Used when the doer is unknown, irrelevant, or when you want to emphasize the object or result.
>     
>     - _Structure:_ `Object (Receiver) + Form of 'Be' + Past Participle (V3) + [by Subject]`
>         
>     - _Example:_ **The manuscript** _was written_ **by the scribe**.
>         
> 
> ### Fundamental Rules of Conversion
> 
> 1. **Move the Object to the Subject Position:** The direct object of the active sentence becomes the new subject of the passive sentence.
>     
> 2. **Add a Form of "To Be":** Insert the correct auxiliary verb `be` (_am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been_) matching the tense of the active verb and the number of the new subject.
>     
> 3. **Change Main Verb to Past Participle (V3):** The primary verb always converts to its third form (e.g., _write_ $\rightarrow$ _written_, _break_ $\rightarrow$ _broken_).
>     
> 4. **Add the Agent (Optional):** Attach `by + original subject` at the end if the doer needs to be specified. Drop it if the doer is obvious or unknown (e.g., _"My keys were stolen"_).
>     
>

|**Past Simple**|`V2`|`was/were + V3`|She edited the report.|The report **was edited** by her.|
|**Past Continuous**|`was/were + V-ing`|`was/were + being + V3`|She was editing the report.|The report **was being edited** by her.|

|**Future Simple**|`will + V1`|`will + be + V3`|She will edit the report.|The report **will be edited** by her.|
